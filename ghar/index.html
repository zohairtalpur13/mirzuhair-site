Ghar, گھر: home

Draw me the home you grew up in. Not from a photograph, from memory: where the doors were, where the light came in, where you slept. Then tell me about one thing that was in it.

Every drawing becomes one page of a shared book. The rooms will be the wrong size and the walls won't meet, and that is the point. A plan drawn from memory shows what a person kept.

01

Four things to draw

The same four instructions appear in the printed kit and on this page, so every page in the book is built the same way.

  1. The walls

    Start at the front door and walk through the house in your head. Draw each wall as you reach it.

  2. The openings

    Add the doors you used and the windows you remember. Leave out the ones you don't.

  3. Where you slept

    Put a star where you slept. Every page in the book has exactly one star.

  4. One object

    Name one thing that was in the house, and write one sentence about it. The object is the page's title.

02

Draw your home

Drag from dot to dot to draw walls. Choose a stamp to place doors, windows, a bed or your star. Nothing you draw leaves your browser unless you download it.

Notes

How the project works, and what it keeps.

  • Private by design The drawing tool runs in your browser. Nothing is uploaded or stored; a page only leaves your computer if you download it and send it.
  • One rule for every page Four instructions, one grid, one star and one object, so pages by strangers can sit in the same book.
  • The example The plan behind “Show an example” is one I drew to test the tool. It is not anyone's home. The book is made of contributions only.
  • References Participatory publications from the Parsons BFA Communication Design thesis shows: a book built from windows photographed around the world, and an archive readers arrange themselves.
