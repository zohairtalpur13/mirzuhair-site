A visual response to a prompt

What is a line,
and what can it do?

The English word line comes from the Latin linea, a thread of linen. Before it was a mark on paper it was a thing you could hold, pull and tie.

This answer comes in two halves. Part I is nine computed lines, made in collaboration with an AI (Claude): nine verbs, each one live. Move, drag or tap. Part II is drawn: a story I made by hand in 2021, out of my own curiosity, starting each frame from a single line. One half is lines that follow rules, the other is lines that follow a hand.

Part I

Computed lines

Nine plates, made with Claude. Each is a small program that does one thing a line can do.

Part II

Drawn lines

Six frames in pencil, by hand, 2021. Each started from a single line.

10 · Become

A story from six lines

I'm a visual artist, and what I love most is telling stories. In 2021, out of curiosity, I set myself a small challenge: six empty frames, one dark line in each, and no plan. Could a single line be enough to start a story?

It was. The first line became a python curled below a wall; one became a bridge, one a head with three hairs. That head became Baldy Lock, an ordinary guy wondering whether he had reached his dream or lost it. I wrote the script in my notebook in English and Urdu, the way I would plan a short film.

One line, four jobs: the line, the python, the bridge, Baldy Lock. Move sideways to scrub

  1. Scene 1 Narration

    Once upon a time there lived an ordinary guy named Baldy Lock, with three hairs. He was standing on a pillar of the Dream Wall, wondering whether his dream had been achieved, or lost.

    [ violin music playing in the background ]

    The starting line: a deep U. It became the gap under the wall, and the python lying in it.

  2. Scene 2 Close shot of Baldy Lock

    آج پھر آیا ہوں اس پل پہ، جس پل چلا تھا اک خواب لے کے
    وہی ہے وہ سانپ، کہ تھا جس سے ڈر وہ جو آج ہے
    کیا پایا اُس خواب کو پا کے، جو چلا تھا لے کے اس پل سے

    "I'm back again on this bridge, with the dream I had when I first came. This is the same python, and the same fear I feel today. What have I achieved after finding that dream? And how did I walk this bridge?"

    The starting line: a closed curve. It became his head.

  3. Scene 3 He gazes at the village where he was born, still smiling like a mother

    دیکھ تو سکتا ہوں تجھے، پر تو بڑی دور ہے
    آج بھی وہ ڈر ہے اس پل سے اور اس سانپ سے
    اور یوں نہ ہو کہ گر جاؤں، اور گرتے ہوئے سوچوں: ہائے، خواب اب بھی دور ہے

    "I can see you, but you're too far. I am scared of this bridge, and of that python. What if I fall, and while falling I wonder: now my dream is far too."

    The starting line: a branch. It became the edge of the wall and the start of the bridge.

  4. Scene 4 Bird's-eye view

    A cemetery between east and south, with a few houses. East and west are all covered by a river with a big snake in it, and only a bridge as a passage.

    [ the violin continues ]

    The starting line: two long parallels. They became the only bridge across.

  5. Scene 5 Worm's-eye view · Narration

    He took the first step with bravery, and with a jug full of fear. With that first step, he was on his way to the village.

    [ excited orchestra in the background ]

    The starting lines: a long bent stroke and a loop. They became his raised arm and his head, mid-stride.

  6. Scene 6 Baldy Lock, relaxed, gazing at the elite society that was his dream

    "It is only now I have realised: peace is not connected with the dream. A restless man in a mansion doesn't have a home, only a house."

    The end.

    The starting lines: two broken strokes and a tall oval. They became the terraces of the city he wanted, and the back of his head.

Looking back, that sheet was asking the same question as this study. A line can be a wall, a snake, a bridge or a person. What it can't do on its own is decide which. That part is the drawing.

11 · Your turn

Here is a line.
What will it become?

This is the exercise I set myself in 2021, handed on to you. The red line is yours now; every visitor gets a different one. Draw around it until it becomes something: a creature, a road, a person, a place. The drawing is the answer.

line /laɪn/ noun

  1. a long narrow mark or band
  2. a length of cord or thread
  3. a row of written or printed words
  4. a limit or boundary
  5. a direction, a course of action

From Latin linea, "linen thread", from linum, flax.

Working notes

This was a quick study, a few hours of work, not a finished piece. I brought the prompt, the Baldy Lock sheet and the textile thread that runs through my practice. Claude and I made a list of every verb that could follow "a line can…" and kept the nine that looked most different on the page. Then we built them as small programs.

The rules were simple. One ink and one red. No fills unless the line earns them, as in Enclose. Each plate had to show its idea without a caption, so the captions were written last.

Putting the two halves side by side taught me the most. A computed line can do almost anything, but only what it's told. My pencil lines in 2021 started from six bare lines, and deciding what each one became was the whole work. I think that choice is what a designer adds.

A line is the smallest
decision a designer makes.
