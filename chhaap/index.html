A block-printing story

Chhaap چھاپ

Chhaap, چھاپ: the print

Every cloth remembers the house it came from.

Scroll, and I will tell you how.

I · The house

A house of lanterns, deer and doorways

In Hyderabad, Sindh, there is a house full of ornament: coloured lanterns, stags above the doors, carved doorways, painted ceilings. I studied it room by room, and I drew what I found by hand.

Choose one thing from the house. It will become your block.

Urdu: lalten, lantern · darwaza, doorway · hiran, deer · chhat, ceiling

II · The block

Carved in reverse

A block printer cuts the drawing into a block of wood. Everything is carved backwards, so that when the block meets the cloth, the drawing reads the right way round.

The lines stand up from the wood. Whatever is cut away will stay empty on the cloth.

III · The press

Press by press

Now the cloth. Tap anywhere on it to press your block. Each press lands a little differently, a little lighter, a little off. That is how you know a hand made it.

No presses yet.

IV · The dye

Madder, then indigo

The printed lines hold. Then the colour comes: first madder, into the heart of every print.

Then indigo, into everything around it. Keep scrolling to dye the cloth.

Madder is a red from a root; indigo, a blue from a leaf.

madderindigo

V · The water

Washed

The cloth goes into the water. Whatever the dye did not hold lets go and floats away. What stays, stays for good.

VI · The sun

Dried in the sun

It is laid out to dry, and the wind moves through it. The colours settle. The house is in the cloth now.

Epilogue

Take the cloth home

A lantern, a doorway, a deer, a ceiling. Every cloth remembers the house it came from. This one is yours.

Notes

  • The drawings The four blocks are my own line drawings from my research at the Talpur haveli in Hyderabad, Sindh. The photographs in the first chapter are from the same research.
  • The process Block printing in Sindh, including ajrak, takes many more steps than this story shows. I kept four moments: the press, the dye, the water and the sun. This is an homage, not a record of a real workshop and not a copy of any traditional block.
  • Nothing leaves your screen The cloth is drawn in your browser. It is only saved if you download it.
  • The research This project began with the interactive and generative work in student portfolios at Parsons, RISD and CalArts, and asks what that way of working looks like when it starts from a house in Sindh.
