Speculative magazine · Art direction · Objects · Motion · Web

Every cover starts in the bin.

Virgil Abloh built a mythology out of ordinary things: a shopping bag called “Sculpture”, a receipt woven into a rug, a plate printed with one word. He changed them by three per cent and put them in quotation marks. SCRAP takes the most ordinary thing in any design studio, the crumpled draft, and gives it the same treatment.

Each of the six issues is built on one of his ideas, and each cover is a single object cast from a thrown-away page: in porcelain, black glaze, blown glass and gilded brass. The covers arrive the way drafts do, as paper balls, and open flat with their creases still showing. The fold is part of the finish.

01–06 The issues

E1–E5 Editorials

SCRAP 01 · The readymade · p.14

The most worthless thing in the studio

Every design begins with pages that don’t survive. They are balled up, thrown at the bin and missed. Abloh’s first move was always to take the thing nobody valued and look at it again. Here the draft is cast in porcelain and fired twice; the second firing lays gold along every crease, so the object records exactly how it was thrown away.

Start with what exists. Change it by three per cent.

SCRAP 02 · The edit · p.32

Three per cent

A carrier bag is the first luxury object most people own. Leave its shape alone, cast it in porcelain so it can never carry anything again, and add one red zip tie. That tie is the whole intervention: the 3% that tells you someone decided this was worth keeping.

SCRAP 03 · Unfinished · p.48

Mended in gold

Perfection is a trap, so this plate arrives already broken. Every fracture is filled with gold in the Japanese kintsugi way, which treats repair as part of an object’s history rather than something to hide. It answers one of Abloh’s last pieces, a plate carrying a single word.

The damage becomes the decoration.

SCRAP 04 · Two readers · p.66

Tourist / Purist

Abloh said he designed for two people at once: the tourist, who loves how a thing looks, and the purist, who knows where it came from. So the same crumpled page is cast twice from one mould. The tourist’s is raw bisque, soft as chalk. The purist’s is black glaze with gold only on the sharpest folds.

SCRAP 06 · The body · p.90

Worn like skin

Schiaparelli treated gold as part of the body: lungs, ears, eyes, cast in gilded brass. Abloh turned industrial hardware into jewellery. SCRAP’s first jewellery borrows from both. The draft is crumpled, cast in brass, gilded, and hung from a single hook, sold one at a time, like a thought you keep.

From the bin to the vitrine.

+ SCRAP HOME

An object line cast from the magazine’s own waste, sold the way fashion houses sell ceramics and books beside their clothes.

+ The invitation

No ink. The launch invitation is blind-embossed on heavy cotton paper, so the words only appear when light rakes across them. It arrives in a crumpled glassine envelope, the one thing in the set that was meant to be thrown away, closed with a gilded crumpled seal.

+ The Bin Room

The launch exhibition. A white room with the floor ankle-deep in crumpled paper and one clear path through it. The covers hang in a perfect line on the far wall; the objects stand on plaster plinths above the mess. Visitors walk through the waste to reach the work, which is the whole idea of the magazine in one room.

+ Research map

The research behind SCRAP is organised as a knowledge graph: 69 ideas, 89 links, eight clusters, covering Abloh’s methods and strategy alongside Margiela, Galliano, McQueen, Saint Laurent and Schiaparelli. Three threads shape the design: the reveal as a table of contents, the found object made precious, and metal worn on the body.
